Section 232 tariffs on steel and auto parts in 2026: what Canadian suppliers should check

By Michelle Liu, CCS, CTCS · Status as of November 13, 2026

U.S. tariffs on steel and automotive goods changed several times in 2026. For a Canadian steel processor or auto parts supplier, the duty on one shipment can now depend on its tariff classification, metal content, where the metal was melted, and whether the part qualifies under USMCA / CUSMA. Here is what changed and what to check.

1. Steel, aluminum and copper: the 2026 tier system

A proclamation signed in April 2026 restructured the Section 232 metals tariffs. Duty is now charged on the full value of the product, and the metal content decides the tier:

Product typeSection 232 rate
Articles made entirely or almost entirely of the metal (for example, steel coil, aluminum sheet)50% of full value
Derivative articles substantially made of the metal25% of full value
Certain metal-intensive industrial equipment and electrical grid equipment15% (through 2027)
Products made abroad entirely with U.S. steel, aluminum or copper10%
Products with 15% or less metal contentNo longer subject to the Section 232 metals tariffs

A further proclamation signed on June 1, 2026 (effective June 8, 2026) adjusted the product annexes. Among other changes, it lowered the threshold for a product to count as made "entirely" from U.S. metal from 95% to 85%, added items such as steel racks, and set special content-based rules for certain USMCA-qualifying goods from Canada and Mexico.

2. Auto parts: the 25% Section 232 tariff

  • A 25% Section 232 tariff on covered automobile parts has applied since May 3, 2025.
  • USMCA-qualifying parts are not subject to this tariff until the U.S. Commerce Department sets up a process to apply it only to their non-U.S. content. As of the latest update I checked (October 2026), that process had not been established.
  • U.S. producers can ask to add more parts to the list during windows that open four times a year (January, April, July and October). Check the list each quarter.

This makes USMCA / CUSMA documentation directly valuable: a supplier that can prove its parts qualify may avoid the 25% duty. See my USMCA / CUSMA rules of origin guide for steel and auto parts.

3. Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods

In August 2026 the United States imposed 50% tariffs under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 on about $27.6 billion of Canadian goods. Canada responded with the United States Surtax Order (2026), in force September 8, 2026, which applies surtaxes of 15%, 25% or 50% to listed U.S.-origin goods. Check whether your products appear on either list, and how the measures stack with Section 232.

4. Canada's surtaxes on U.S. steel and aluminum

Canada also applies surtaxes on many U.S. steel and aluminum products. During 2026, Canada narrowed the broad remission (relief) that had been available to manufacturers and the automotive sector. Case-by-case remission through the Department of Finance Canada remains possible in specific situations, for example when goods cannot be sourced in Canada or when exceptional circumstances would cause severe economic harm. Strong evidence is essential.

What Canadian steel and auto parts suppliers should check now

  1. Classification: confirm the HTSUS code and the Chapter 99 numbers that apply to each product.
  2. Metal content: know the steel, aluminum and copper content and value of each product. This now decides the Section 232 tier.
  3. Melt-and-pour and smelt-and-cast records: keep mill certificates that show where the metal was produced.
  4. USMCA / CUSMA proof: make sure certifications and supporting records are current, especially for auto parts.
  5. Stacking: map how Section 232, Section 338 and regular duties combine on each entry.
  6. U.S. goods returned: if U.S.-origin steel is processed in Canada and returned, review whether HTSUS Chapter 98 provisions (such as 9802) apply.
  7. Canadian side: review surtax exposure on U.S. steel inputs and whether a Finance Canada remission request is justified.
