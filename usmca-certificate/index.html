Field guide

8. Origin criterion (USMCA Article 4.2): A – wholly obtained or produced entirely in a USMCA country; B – produced using non-originating materials that satisfy the product-specific rule (Annex 4-B); C – produced exclusively from originating materials; D – produced using non-originating parts that cannot meet the tariff shift only because they are classified with the good, and the RVC requirement is met.

9. Producer: YES if the certifier is the producer of the good, otherwise NO.

10. Method of qualification: TS – tariff shift; TV – regional value content by transaction value; NC – regional value content by net cost; NO – criterion A or C.

11. Country of origin: ISO code US, CA or MX.

De minimis (USMCA Article 4.12): a good can still qualify if the non-originating materials that fail the tariff shift are worth no more than 10% of the good's transaction value (FOB) or total cost, and it meets all other requirements. A good subject to a regional value content rule does not need to meet it if all non-originating materials are no more than 10% of that value. Exceptions apply (Annex 4-A, e.g. certain dairy, sugar, juice and coffee materials, and materials in the same subheading as a Chapter 1–27 good), and textiles (Chapters 50–63) use a 10% weight test with a 7% limit on elastomeric content. A good that relies on de minimis is criterion B (or D), not C.

Disclaimer

Depending on the product, the product-specific rule of origin is different. Please have a customs compliance professional or consultant review the result before you rely on it.

This tool provides a preliminary analysis (a rule-based calculation, or an optional AI-assisted review) for general information only. It is not legal advice, not a binding ruling and not a decision of U.S. Customs and Border Protection, the Canada Border Services Agency or Mexican customs. USMCA/CUSMA product-specific rules of origin (Annex 4-B and its Appendix for automotive goods) vary by tariff classification, and the result depends entirely on the accuracy and completeness of the product description, HS code, bill of materials, values and supplier origin information you provide. Automated analysis can be wrong: the free checker does not verify your HS classification or your suppliers' origin claims, and its built-in rules cover only some chapters.

The person who signs a certification of origin is responsible for its accuracy and must keep the records that support it (generally at least five years). Incorrect certifications can lead to duty assessments, penalties and loss of preferential treatment. Use of this tool does not create a client relationship. The author accepts no liability for any loss arising from its use.

The USMCA has no mandatory certificate form; a certification is valid when it contains the minimum data elements in USMCA Annex 5-A. The layout used here follows the commonly used template format.
